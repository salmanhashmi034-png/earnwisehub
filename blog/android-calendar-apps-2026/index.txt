<p>A reliable calendar app is one of the most useful tools on any Android phone. Whether you need basic event scheduling or a full planning system with task integration, these 10 apps cover the range.</p>

<h2>1. Google Calendar</h2>
<p>Google Calendar is the default choice for most Android users and handles most scheduling needs well. It integrates with Gmail to automatically detect events in emails, supports shared calendars and works well with other Google services.</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Reliable, free and deeply integrated with Android and Google services.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Most Android users, especially those using Google Workspace or Gmail.</p>

<h2>2. Microsoft Outlook</h2>
<p>Microsoft Outlook combines email and calendar in one app. If you use a Microsoft 365 or Exchange account, Outlook handles both well. The calendar view is clean and it integrates with Teams for meetings.</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Combined email and calendar, excellent for Microsoft 365 users.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Professional users who rely on Microsoft 365 or Outlook email.</p>

<h2>3. Any.do</h2>
<p>Any.do combines a to-do list and calendar in one app. It has a clean interface and a daily planning feature that helps you review and prioritise your day each morning.</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Task and calendar integration in one simple interface.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Users who want tasks and calendar events in one place.</p>

<h2>4. Fantastical</h2>
<p>Fantastical is known for its natural language input — you can type "lunch with Sarah next Tuesday at 1pm" and it creates the event correctly. It has a polished interface and useful week and month views. Requires a subscription for full features.</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Natural language input is fast and the interface is well-designed.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Users who create many events and want the fastest possible input method.</p>

<h2>5. Zoho Calendar</h2>
<p>Zoho Calendar is a free calendar app with a clean interface that works well as a standalone calendar. It integrates with other Zoho apps for business users.</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Generous free tier with a clean, distraction-free interface.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Zoho users or those wanting a simple free alternative to Google Calendar.</p>

<h2>6. Business Calendar 2</h2>
<p>Business Calendar 2 offers a highly customisable calendar experience with multiple views, colour coding and task integration. The free version has good functionality; a paid version removes ads and adds extra features.</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Highly customisable views and good task integration.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Power users who want more control over their calendar display.</p>

<h2>7. Proton Calendar</h2>
<p>Proton Calendar from the makers of ProtonMail offers end-to-end encrypted calendar events. If privacy is a priority, it keeps your schedule details away from third parties.</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Events are end-to-end encrypted — Proton cannot read your calendar data.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Privacy-conscious users who want an encrypted calendar.</p>

<h2>8. DigiCal Calendar Agenda</h2>
<p>DigiCal offers multiple calendar views including a weather integration that shows forecast alongside your events. It has a clean design and supports multiple accounts.</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Weather integration alongside events is useful for planning outdoor activities.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Users who want weather information visible alongside their schedule.</p>

<h2>9. TimeTree</h2>
<p>TimeTree is designed specifically for shared calendars — useful for families, couples or small teams who need to coordinate schedules. It supports comments on events and has a messaging feature.</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Designed around sharing — better for coordinating with others than most calendar apps.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Families, couples or small groups who need a shared scheduling tool.</p>

<h2>10. Simple Calendar Pro</h2>
<p>Simple Calendar Pro is an open-source calendar app with no ads and no internet permissions required for basic use. It is lightweight and privacy-friendly.</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Open source, privacy-focused and works offline without requiring internet permissions.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Privacy-conscious users who want a simple offline calendar.</p>

<h2>Conclusion</h2>
<p>Google Calendar is the practical choice for most Android users due to its integration with the rest of the ecosystem. If you use Microsoft 365, Outlook Calendar is similarly well-integrated. For shared scheduling, TimeTree stands out; for privacy, Proton Calendar is worth considering.</p>
