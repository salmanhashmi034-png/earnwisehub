<p>Your Android launcher controls how your home screen looks and works. Most phones come with a manufacturer launcher (Samsung One UI, Pixel Launcher, etc.) but you can replace it with a third-party launcher for more customisation. Here are 10 worth trying in 2026.</p>

<h2>What is a Launcher?</h2>
<p>A launcher is the app that manages your home screen, app drawer and dock. Changing your launcher does not delete apps or data — it only changes the interface you use to access them. You can switch back to the default launcher at any time in your phone settings.</p>

<h2>1. Nova Launcher</h2>
<p>Nova Launcher is one of the most popular third-party launchers. It is highly customisable — icon size, grid size, animations, gestures, app drawer organisation — while remaining stable and fast. The free version is comprehensive; Nova Launcher Prime adds extra features.</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Enormous customisation options with reliable performance.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Users who want full control over their home screen layout and behaviour.</p>

<h2>2. Microsoft Launcher</h2>
<p>Microsoft Launcher integrates Android with Microsoft services — your calendar, to-do list, recent documents and Cortana are accessible from the home screen. It works well for Microsoft 365 users.</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Deep Microsoft 365 integration — useful if your work runs on Microsoft services.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Microsoft 365 users who want their work tools front and centre.</p>

<h2>3. Niagara Launcher</h2>
<p>Niagara takes a minimal approach — a vertical app list with quick access to your most-used apps. It is clean, distraction-reducing and works particularly well on large phones.</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Minimal design that reduces screen clutter and distractions.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Users who want a simple, focused interface rather than a widget-heavy home screen.</p>

<h2>4. Lawnchair</h2>
<p>Lawnchair is an open-source launcher based on the Pixel Launcher. It brings Pixel Launcher features (like Google Feed integration) to non-Pixel phones with added customisation.</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Open source with Pixel-like experience and extra customisation.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Non-Pixel Android users who want the Pixel Launcher experience.</p>

<h2>5. POCO Launcher</h2>
<p>POCO Launcher from Xiaomi organises apps by category automatically. It has a clean design and supports dual app space for devices with the feature.</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Automatic app categorisation makes finding apps easier.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Users who have many apps and want them organised automatically.</p>

<h2>6. Smart Launcher 6</h2>
<p>Smart Launcher categorises and organises apps automatically. It adapts icon colours to your wallpaper and has a focus mode that hides distracting apps.</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Automatic organisation and a focus mode for reducing distractions.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Users who want automated app organisation with distraction management.</p>

<h2>7. Olauncher</h2>
<p>Olauncher is a minimal text-based launcher that shows just a few apps as text on a clean background. It is designed to reduce phone use and promote a less distracting relationship with your device.</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Extremely minimal — intentionally designed to reduce phone addiction.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Users trying to reduce screen time and phone dependence.</p>

<h2>8. Ratio Launcher</h2>
<p>Ratio is a minimal, card-based launcher with a unique design philosophy. It surfaces relevant information (calendar, weather, recent apps) in a clean card-based interface.</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Unique card-based design that surfaces useful information automatically.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Users who want a distinctive, information-first home screen.</p>

<h2>9. Hyperion Launcher</h2>
<p>Hyperion Launcher offers deep customisation with a particular focus on icon pack support and visual polish. It is well-regarded by users who enjoy icon packs and themed setups.</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Excellent icon pack support for visually themed setups.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Users who invest time in themed home screen setups with custom icon packs.</p>

<h2>10. TouchWiz / One UI Home (Samsung)</h2>
<p>For Samsung users, the built-in One UI Home launcher is already well-optimised for Samsung devices. Before installing a third-party launcher, consider whether One UI already meets your needs — it is increasingly capable.</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Deep integration with Samsung features and widgets.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Samsung users who do not need extra customisation beyond what One UI provides.</p>

<h2>Conclusion</h2>
<p>Nova Launcher is the most customisable choice. Niagara is the best for minimalism. Microsoft Launcher suits Microsoft 365 users. Olauncher is worth trying if you want to reduce screen time. All of these are reversible — try one and if you prefer your original launcher, switch back in Settings.</p>
