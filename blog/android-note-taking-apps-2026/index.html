10 Best Android Note-Taking Apps in 2026 for Notes, Ideas & Organisation

The right note-taking app helps you capture ideas quickly, organise information clearly and find what you need when you need it. These 10 Android apps cover everything from quick notes to complex knowledge management.

By EarnWiseHub Editorial TeamPublished September 6, 2026

Note-taking apps range from simple tools for capturing quick thoughts to sophisticated systems for organising complex information. The right choice depends on how you work and what you need to do with your notes. Here are 10 strong options for Android in 2026.

1. Google Keep

Google Keep is simple, fast and free. It supports text notes, voice notes, images, drawings and checklists. Notes can be colour-coded, labelled and shared. It integrates with Google Docs.

Why it is useful: Very fast to open and capture a thought. No setup required.

Best for: Quick notes, reminders and simple checklists.

2. Microsoft OneNote

OneNote organises notes into notebooks, sections and pages. It handles text, images, audio recordings, drawings and attachments. Syncs across devices with a Microsoft account.

Why it is useful: Flexible structure and handles multimedia notes well.

Best for: Detailed notes with mixed content types, especially for Microsoft 365 users.

3. Notion

Notion is more than a note app — it is a workspace that combines notes, databases, wikis, task boards and calendars. The learning curve is steeper than simpler apps, but it is highly flexible.

Why it is useful: Can replace many apps once you have set it up for your workflow.

Best for: Users who want a single tool for notes, tasks and project management.

4. Obsidian

Obsidian stores notes as plain Markdown files on your device. Its standout feature is bidirectional linking — notes can link to each other, creating a personal knowledge graph. It is popular with writers, researchers and developers.

Why it is useful: Your notes are plain text files you own completely — no vendor lock-in.

Best for: People who write extensively and want to connect ideas across notes.

5. Evernote

Evernote is one of the original note-taking apps. It handles text, images, web clips, PDFs and scanned documents. The free tier is now quite limited; the paid tier is more practical for heavy users.

Why it is useful: Strong web clipping and document scanning features.

Best for: Users who clip a lot of web content and want it organised alongside their notes.

6. Bear

Bear is a Markdown-based note app with a clean, focused design. It supports tags for organisation and has a pleasant writing experience. Note: Bear is primarily an iOS/Mac app — the Android version may have more limited functionality.

Why it is useful: Beautiful writing interface with Markdown support.

Best for: Writers who want a clean, distraction-free writing environment.

7. Standard Notes

Standard Notes is a free, open-source, encrypted note app. It stores notes securely with end-to-end encryption. The free tier covers basic text notes; paid tiers add formatting and extensions.

Why it is useful: Strong privacy and security — notes are end-to-end encrypted.

Best for: Privacy-conscious users who want encrypted notes.

8. Joplin

Joplin is a free, open-source Markdown note app with end-to-end encrypted sync. You control where your notes sync — Dropbox, OneDrive, Nextcloud or your own server.

Why it is useful: Open source, encrypted and you control where data is stored.

Best for: Technical users who want full control over their note storage.

9. Simplenote

Simplenote is exactly what the name says — a simple, fast, free note app. Text only. Syncs across all platforms. Version history is included.

Why it is useful: Fast, free, cross-platform and includes version history.

Best for: Users who only need text notes and want the simplest possible tool.

10. Zoho Notebook

Zoho Notebook organises notes into visual notebooks that look like actual notebooks. It supports text, audio, checklists, photos and sketches — all for free.

Why it is useful: Visual organisation with a generous free tier.

Best for: Users who want a visually appealing free alternative to Evernote.

Conclusion

For most people, Google Keep or Microsoft OneNote covers everyday note-taking needs at no cost. If you write extensively and want to connect ideas, Obsidian or Notion offer more depth. Most of these apps offer free tiers — try a couple before committing to one system.
