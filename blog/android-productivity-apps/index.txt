<p>Productivity apps on Android range from simple to-do lists to powerful project management tools. This guide covers 10 apps that are genuinely useful for improving daily organisation and output.</p>

<h2>1. Todoist</h2>
<p>Todoist is a task management app with a clean interface and natural language input. You can type "submit report Friday at 3pm" and it creates the task with the correct date and time automatically.</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Reliable across platforms, good for both personal tasks and project tracking.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> People who want a straightforward task manager that syncs across devices.</p>

<h2>2. Notion</h2>
<p>Notion combines notes, databases, wikis and task boards in one app. It is flexible enough to use as a simple note app or a complex project management system.</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Extremely versatile — one app can replace several others.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> People comfortable with setting up their own system and who need more than a basic to-do list.</p>

<h2>3. Microsoft To Do</h2>
<p>Microsoft To Do is a clean, free task manager that integrates well with Outlook and Microsoft 365. It includes a "My Day" view that helps you focus on what is important today.</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Free, simple and works well if you use Microsoft services.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Microsoft 365 users looking for a task manager that integrates with their existing tools.</p>

<h2>4. Google Keep</h2>
<p>Google Keep is a simple note-taking app that supports text, voice notes, images and checklists. Notes can be colour-coded and shared.</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Fast to open and capture a quick thought. Integrates with Google Docs.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Quick notes, shopping lists and reminders.</p>

<h2>5. Forest</h2>
<p>Forest is a focus app that uses a gamified approach — you plant a virtual tree that grows while you stay off your phone. Leaving the app kills the tree.</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Effective for people who struggle with phone distractions during focused work.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Students and anyone who needs help maintaining phone-free focus sessions.</p>

<h2>6. Toggl Track</h2>
<p>Toggl Track is a time-tracking app that lets you record how you spend your working hours. It is useful for freelancers who bill by the hour and for anyone wanting to understand where their time goes.</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Simple one-tap time tracking with clear reports.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Freelancers, consultants and remote workers who track billable hours.</p>

<h2>7. Trello</h2>
<p>Trello uses a board and card system (Kanban) for organising projects visually. You can move cards between columns like "To Do," "In Progress" and "Done."</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Visual project tracking that works well for both personal and team projects.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> People who prefer visual organisation over list-based systems.</p>

<h2>8. 1Password</h2>
<p>1Password is a password manager that stores your passwords securely and autofills them on apps and websites. Using a password manager means you can use unique strong passwords everywhere without remembering them all.</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Strong security practice that saves time logging in.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Anyone who reuses passwords or struggles to remember different credentials.</p>

<h2>9. Grammarly Keyboard</h2>
<p>Grammarly's keyboard checks spelling, grammar and clarity in real time across any app where you type. It works across messages, emails and documents.</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Catches errors as you type without needing to copy text into a separate tool.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Writers, professionals and anyone who communicates in written English regularly.</p>

<h2>10. Google Calendar</h2>
<p>Google Calendar is one of the most reliable calendar apps for Android. It handles events, reminders, shared calendars and integrates with Gmail for automatic event detection.</p>
<p><strong>Why it is useful:</strong> Reliable, free and integrates well with other Google services.</p>
<p><strong>Best for:</strong> Anyone who needs a dependable calendar app, particularly Google Workspace users.</p>

<h2>Conclusion</h2>
<p>The right productivity app depends on how you naturally work. Try a few of these — most offer free tiers — and stick with the ones that genuinely fit your habits rather than adding complexity. The best productivity app is the one you actually use consistently.</p>
