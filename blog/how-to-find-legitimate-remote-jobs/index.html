How to Find Legitimate Remote Jobs: A Practical Guide

Finding genuine remote work among a sea of vague listings requires knowing where to look and what to look for. This guide covers the best job boards, red flags to watch for, and how to improve your applications.

By EarnWiseHub Editorial TeamPublished August 20, 2026

The remote job market is large but also noisy — filled with vague listings, misleading postings and, at the scam end, fake opportunities. This guide gives you a clear process for finding and vetting genuine remote positions.

  2. What makes a genuine listing
  3. Red flags to watch for

  5. Finding entry-level remote work

1. Where to look

These are reputable sources for remote job listings. Inclusion here is based on their general reputation as job boards — always research any employer independently before providing personal information or accepting any role.

General job boards with remote filters

  • LinkedIn Jobs — Filter by "Remote" under location. One of the highest-volume sources of legitimate remote listings.
  • Indeed — Large volume, filter by remote. Quality varies widely; vetting is important.
  • Glassdoor — Job listings alongside company reviews from current and former employees.

Remote-specific job boards

  • We Work Remotely (weworkremotely.com) — One of the larger dedicated remote job boards. Listings tend to be tech, design and marketing-focused.
  • Remote.co — Curated remote listings with company profiles.
  • FlexJobs — Subscription-based service with manually vetted remote and flexible listings. The subscription cost is worth considering.
  • RemoteOK — Tech-heavy remote job board.
  • Himalayas — Remote jobs with salary transparency.

Company career pages

Many fully remote companies list jobs exclusively on their own career pages. If there are specific companies whose values and products interest you, check their careers section directly and sign up for job alerts where offered.

2. What makes a genuine listing

Legitimate remote job listings typically:

  • Name the company clearly (not just "a fast-growing startup")
  • Describe specific responsibilities in detail
  • List genuine requirements (skills, experience, tools)
  • State a salary range or salary band
  • Explain the hiring process
  • Have a real company website that matches the listing
  • Direct you to apply through their official site or ATS (applicant tracking system)

3. Red flags to watch for

See our full guide on identifying online job scams for more detail. Key red flags in job listings:

  • No company name or verifiable company information
  • Unusually high pay for very low skill requirements
  • Requests for payment to access work or "training materials"
  • Requests for personal or financial information very early in the process
  • Offers that do not require any interview or application process
  • Vague descriptions like "social media evaluator" or "home-based opportunity" with no specifics
  • Contact via WhatsApp, Telegram or personal email rather than a company email address
  • Grammar and spelling errors throughout the listing

4. How to apply effectively

Remote applications are competitive. Stand out by:

  • Tailoring your CV/resume: Generic applications perform poorly. Address the specific requirements in each job description.
  • Writing a strong cover letter: Many remote employers read cover letters carefully, particularly for fully remote roles where they cannot assess you in person.
  • Demonstrating remote-readiness: Mention any experience of remote work, relevant tools you are familiar with, and how you manage your time independently.
  • Researching the company: References to specific company details in your application show genuine interest.
  • Following the application instructions: Remote employers sometimes add small tests to their job descriptions (e.g., "include the word 'remote' in your subject line") to filter applications. Following these instructions exactly helps.

5. Finding entry-level remote work

Entry-level remote work is more limited than experienced-level, but it exists. Categories where entry-level remote positions are available include:

  • Customer support and live chat
  • Data entry and administrative support
  • Content writing and editing (where you can demonstrate ability)
  • Social media scheduling and community management
  • Research and data collection tasks
  • Online tutoring (if you have subject knowledge)

Starting with contract or part-time remote work is a valid route to building a track record for full-time remote roles later.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need experience to get a remote job?

Most remote roles require relevant experience, just like office roles. Entry-level remote positions exist but competition for them is high. Building transferable skills (digital tools, written communication, self-management) improves your candidacy.

Is it faster to find remote work on freelance platforms?

Freelance platforms (Upwork, Fiverr) are different from remote employment — they provide contract work rather than employment. Both are valid options; the right choice depends on whether you prefer employment or self-employment. See our comparison guide: Freelancing vs Remote Employment.

Conclusion

Finding legitimate remote work is a matter of knowing the right sources, vetting listings carefully and applying professionally. The process is not significantly different from finding any job — but the remote filter increases competition, making application quality more important than volume.
