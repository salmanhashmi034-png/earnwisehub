<p>Online job scams are a widespread problem, and they are not always obvious. Some are crude — poorly written offers promising unrealistic pay — but others are sophisticated and can initially appear legitimate. This guide covers the most important warning signs.</p>

<h2>Table of Contents</h2>

<h2 id="common-types">1. Common types of online job scams</h2>

<h3>Reshipping scams</h3>
<p>You are offered a work-from-home role as a "package handler" or "shipping coordinator." In practice, you receive stolen goods and forward them elsewhere, making you unknowingly complicit in fraud. Legitimate businesses do not operate this way.</p>

<h3>Money transfer/check scams</h3>
<p>A "company" sends you a cheque and asks you to deposit it and send a portion to someone else. The cheque later bounces, leaving you liable for the full amount. No legitimate employer sends you money to forward to third parties.</p>

<h3>Fake mystery shopper jobs</h3>
<p>Unsolicited offers to become a mystery shopper, often requiring you to purchase gift cards or wire transfers as part of a "task." Legitimate mystery shopping companies do exist, but they do not recruit unsolicited by email and do not ask you to send money.</p>

<h3>Work-from-home assembly jobs</h3>
<p>You are asked to purchase a "kit" to assemble products at home, promised payment per item. After purchasing and completing work, payment is refused — typically because items allegedly "don't meet quality standards."</p>

<h3>Data entry and microtask scams</h3>
<p>You pay a registration or training fee to access a supposed data entry job that either does not exist or pays pennies. Legitimate data entry platforms (like Clickworker) are free to join and do not charge to access tasks.</p>

<h3>Fake remote employer impersonation</h3>
<p>Scammers impersonate real companies — creating fake job postings on legitimate job boards or messaging people directly on LinkedIn. They conduct fake "interviews" via text or chat, make quick offers, and ask for personal information or equipment deposits.</p>

<h2 id="warning-signs">2. Warning signs to look for</h2>

<h3>Before applying</h3>
<ul>
  <li>No identifiable company name, or a company name that cannot be verified</li>
  <li>Pay that is unusually high relative to the skill level required</li>
  <li>Vague job description ("data entry," "social media assistant," "home-based opportunity")</li>
  <li>Spelling and grammar errors throughout the listing</li>
  <li>The listing was forwarded to you unsolicited (email, WhatsApp, Telegram)</li>
</ul>

<h3>During the "hiring" process</h3>
<ul>
  <li>No real interview, or an "interview" conducted only via text/chat</li>
  <li>Job offered within hours of application with no verification of your credentials</li>
  <li>Requests for personal identification, bank details or National Insurance/Social Security number before a contract is signed</li>
  <li>Requests for any upfront payment (equipment deposit, training fee, background check fee)</li>
  <li>Communication only from personal email addresses (gmail.com, yahoo.com), not company domains</li>
  <li>Pressure to accept quickly or "before the role is filled"</li>
</ul>

<h3>Red flags in contract/offer</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Vague or non-existent contract</li>
  <li>Company address that does not match their claimed location</li>
  <li>Requests to receive money in your bank account and forward it elsewhere</li>
  <li>Gift card payment requests for any purpose</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="what-to-do">3. What to do if you encounter a scam</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Stop all contact immediately</strong> — do not send any documents, payment or personal information</li>
  <li><strong>Do not engage with follow-up messages</strong> — scammers often increase pressure if they sense hesitation</li>
  <li><strong>If you have shared bank details:</strong> contact your bank immediately to discuss protective measures</li>
  <li><strong>If you have sent money:</strong> contact your bank or payment service immediately — recovery is not guaranteed but prompt action improves chances</li>
  <li><strong>Report the listing</strong> to the platform where you found it</li>
  <li><strong>Report to relevant authorities</strong> (see below)</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="reporting">4. How to report</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>United States:</strong> Report to the Federal Trade Commission at <a href="https://reportfraud.ftc.gov" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">reportfraud.ftc.gov</a> and the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center at <a href="https://ic3.gov" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">ic3.gov</a></li>
  <li><strong>United Kingdom:</strong> Report to Action Fraud at <a href="https://www.actionfraud.police.uk" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">actionfraud.police.uk</a></li>
  <li><strong>Canada:</strong> Report to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at <a href="https://www.antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca</a></li>
  <li><strong>Australia:</strong> Report to Scamwatch at <a href="https://www.scamwatch.gov.au" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">scamwatch.gov.au</a></li>
</ul>

<h2 id="faq">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3>Are all work-from-home jobs suspicious?</h3>
<p>No. Legitimate remote employment is widespread. The warning signs listed above help distinguish genuine remote roles from fraudulent ones.</p>

<h3>What if a company seems real but something feels wrong?</h3>
<p>Trust the feeling. Independently verify the company (check their official website, look them up on Companies House or equivalent, search for reviews on Glassdoor). Do not proceed until you are satisfied.</p>

<h3>Can I recover money lost to a job scam?</h3>
<p>Recovery is sometimes possible, particularly if payment was made via a bank transfer (contact your bank) or credit card. Cryptocurrency or gift card payments are rarely recoverable. Acting quickly improves chances.</p>

<h2>Conclusion</h2>
<p>The best defence against online job scams is a combination of knowledge and scepticism. Legitimate employers do not charge fees, send you money to forward, or rush you into providing personal information. If something does not feel right, step back and verify independently before proceeding.</p>
