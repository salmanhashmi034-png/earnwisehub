How to Sell Digital Products Online: A Practical Introduction

Selling digital products — templates, guides, printables, presets — is one of the more scalable side-income approaches. This guide covers what types of products sell, where to sell them, and what is realistically involved.

By EarnWiseHub Editorial TeamPublished September 20, 2026

Selling digital products involves creating something once and selling it repeatedly without additional production cost per sale. This makes it one of the more scalable side-income approaches available. However, it requires upfront effort to create quality products and ongoing effort to attract buyers — it is not passive in the setup phase.

Types of digital products that sell

Templates

  • Canva templates (social media kits, presentation templates, branding sets)
  • Notion templates (project trackers, dashboards, planning systems)
  • Excel/Sheets templates (budgets, trackers, planners)
  • Resume and CV templates

Printables and documents

  • Planners and organisers
  • Worksheets and workbooks
  • Wall art printables
  • Educational materials

Guides and eBooks

  • How-to guides in specific niches
  • Resource lists
  • Step-by-step process documents

Creative assets

  • Photography presets (Lightroom, Capture One)
  • Stock photos and illustrations (if you have original work)
  • Sound effects and music (if you produce audio)
  • Fonts and graphics (design expertise required)

Where to sell digital products

Etsy

Etsy is a large marketplace with built-in traffic, particularly for printables, planners, templates and creative assets. It supports digital downloads directly. Fees apply per listing and per sale (check current fee structure on etsy.com).

Advantages: existing buyer traffic. Disadvantages: competitive, fees, platform-dependent income.

Gumroad

Gumroad is a straightforward platform for creators selling digital products directly. It handles payment processing, file delivery and basic analytics. A flat fee applies to transactions (check gumroad.com for current pricing).

Advantages: simple setup, works for a wide range of products. Disadvantages: you need to drive your own traffic — Gumroad does not have marketplace browsing like Etsy.

Your own website with a payment processor

The most independent option. You handle everything yourself — product delivery, payment, customer service. Tools like Shopify, WooCommerce or Lemon Squeezy can facilitate this.

Advantages: full control, no marketplace fees. Disadvantages: requires your own traffic, more setup, ongoing management.

Creating a product that sells

The most common mistake with digital products is creating something nobody is searching for. Research before creating:

  • Search for your product type on Etsy and check what is selling (review counts indicate sales volume)
  • Look for underserved niches — broad templates are highly competitive; niche templates (e.g., "Notion template for independent therapists") have less competition
  • Quality matters significantly — a well-designed, genuinely useful product in a moderately competitive space performs better than a mediocre product in a low-competition space

Pricing

Digital product pricing is not straightforward. Common approaches:

  • Research competitor pricing for similar products
  • Price based on the value to the buyer, not the time it took you to create
  • Test different price points — many sellers find slightly higher prices convert as well as low prices while earning more per sale

Realistic expectations

Digital products are not "set and forget." Building a product shop that generates meaningful income typically involves:

  • Creating multiple products over time (one or two products rarely generates consistent income)
  • Optimising listings with good descriptions, keywords and images
  • Marketing your products (Pinterest, social media, a mailing list)
  • Updating products as tools change (Canva templates may need updating as the platform evolves)

Most sellers take months to see consistent sales. Treat it as a medium-term project, not an immediate income source.

Tax and legal considerations

Digital product income is taxable in most countries. If you are selling internationally, VAT/GST rules on digital goods can be complex. Platforms like Etsy and Gumroad handle tax collection for many markets — verify what each platform handles versus what you are responsible for in your country.

Conclusion

Selling digital products is a legitimate and potentially scalable side income approach, but it requires upfront effort in creation, market research and ongoing promotion. Success comes from creating genuinely useful products, presenting them well, and building an audience over time — not from listing one or two templates and expecting immediate sales.
