UserTesting Review: How It Works, Pay & What Testers Do

UserTesting pays people to record themselves using websites and apps while narrating their experience. This review covers how the platform works, what testers do, payment details and eligibility.

1. What is UserTesting?

UserTesting is a platform that connects businesses wanting feedback on their digital products with members of the public who test those products and record their experience. Companies use this feedback to improve their websites, apps and digital experiences.

2. How it works

  1. You apply to become a tester and complete a practice test to assess your suitability
  2. If approved, you are added to the tester panel
  3. When tests matching your profile become available, you receive a notification
  4. You accept and complete the test within the time window
  5. You record your screen and voice as you complete tasks on a website or app
  6. You answer follow-up questions about your experience
  7. Payment is issued after the client reviews the test

3. Eligibility and requirements

Requirements as of the last check include:

  • Age 18 or older
  • A computer or mobile device capable of screen recording
  • A microphone (for think-aloud narration)
  • Reliable internet connection
  • Passing the qualifying practice test (clarity of communication is assessed)
  • Being located in a country UserTesting supports

UserTesting availability varies by country. Check the current tester signup page at usertesting.com to confirm whether it is accepting testers in your region.

4. Pay and payments

UserTesting pays per completed test. Rates vary by test type and length. Standard tests have historically paid around $10 per 20-minute test, but rates vary and change over time.

Live conversation tests (direct video calls with researchers) typically pay more than recorded tests.

Payment is issued via PayPal, typically several days after test completion.

Always verify current pay rates on UserTesting's official website. The figures in this review reflect publicly available information at the last check date and may not reflect current rates.

5. What tests involve

Typical tests involve:

  • Visiting a website or app
  • Following a scenario (e.g., "You are looking for a pair of running shoes under $80. Show us how you would find and purchase them.")
  • Speaking your thoughts aloud as you navigate (think-aloud protocol)
  • Answering follow-up questions after completing the tasks

Testers are evaluated on the quality of their verbal commentary, clarity of screen recording and whether they follow instructions. Poor-quality submissions can result in lower ratings, which may affect future test opportunities.

6. Pros and limitations

Pros

  • No specialist technical skill required — general computer literacy is sufficient
  • Clear, defined task for each test
  • Pay per test (not per hour, so short tests can be reasonable value)
  • Legitimate platform used by major brands

Limitations

  • Test availability is highly variable — many testers report inconsistent supply
  • Must pass a qualifying test to be accepted
  • Not available in all countries
  • Earnings are supplemental — test volume is not sufficient for primary income
  • Some testers report low test frequency after initial acceptance

Frequently Asked Questions

Is UserTesting legitimate?

Yes. UserTesting is a legitimate and well-established platform used by many major companies for product research. It is not a scam.

How many tests can I expect per month?

This varies considerably and depends on your demographic profile and location. It is not possible to predict a reliable number. Treat it as occasional supplemental income.

What happens if I fail the qualifying test?

If your practice test does not meet the standard required, you may be declined or asked to retry. Check UserTesting's current tester help resources for details.

Conclusion

UserTesting is a legitimate platform for earning supplemental income by providing feedback on digital products. Testers who communicate clearly, follow instructions precisely and deliver consistent quality can earn from it regularly. However, test availability is not guaranteed, and it is best treated as occasional supplemental income rather than a reliable regular earner.
